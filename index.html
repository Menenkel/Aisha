Accountability

You cannot answer for a decision you cannot reconstruct.

Acceptability and transparency are not optional in humanitarian response — they are what earns it legitimacy with the people it serves. Both depend on being able to explain a decision, and AI-derived guidance used without validation quietly removes that ability.

  1. Basis

    Can the source be produced on request?

  2. Review

    Who checked it, and what did they change?

  3. Recourse

    Can the decision be contested by those it affects?

Unverified outputs fail all three — and the failure is recorded nowhere. This lands hardest on national and local partners, who receive these tools downstream with the smallest training budget and no leverage over the vendor.

Control

  • Remove names and identifying details before you enter anything into the tool
  • Get the vendor to confirm in writing they won't store or train on your data
  • A second reviewer checks it, and that review is recorded
  • Agree in advance how long the data is kept before it's deleted

A starting point, not a standard. AISHA calibrates these with each partner and each operating context.

Principles

Five commitments that shape how we work.

Read all 5 principles
  1. 01 — Human-led

    AI strengthens judgement; it doesn't replace it. Staff keep the authority to review, challenge and override.

  2. 02 — Resilient systems

    We strengthen the wider systems AI is layered onto — not just the organisations using it.

  3. 03 — Locally grounded

    We work at the operational layer, with local partners, not just at the policy layer.

  4. 04 — Collaborative

    We reinforce the institutions and coordination mechanisms that already exist, rather than duplicate them.

  5. 05 — Deliberately scaled

    Safeguards match the risk. We retain the ability to pause or stop any intervention.
